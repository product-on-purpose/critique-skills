# 0034 - The v0.1.x exit gate is met, and what it does not say

## TL;DR
- **Decision:** the `v0.1.x` exit gate in [`ROADMAP.md`](../../../ROADMAP.md), "two consecutive weeks
  with no open contract or check defect", is **declared met as of 2026-09-13**. The most conservative
  clock start available is 2026-08-25, which is 19 days, and the issue tracker has stood at zero open
  issues throughout.
- **Second ruling, which the declaration required:** a **site-guard defect does count** as a check
  defect and does reset the clock. The gate says "check defect" without qualification, a site guard
  is a check, and carving one out would make the gate mean "the checks I was thinking of in July".
- **Why it can be ruled honestly now:** the answer does not change the outcome. Every candidate clock
  start, including the site-guard one, clears two weeks. Ruling a definition while it is still free
  is the only time the ruling is not suspected of being chosen to fit.
- **What this does NOT declare:** that `v0.1.x` is finished. **Two of the four verification items the
  version is named for have never been run**: the live planted-failure checks and the tag-guard from
  a scratch clone. The gate measures quiet; it does not measure completion.
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-13
- **Deciders:** Jonathan Prisant

## Context and problem statement

`ROADMAP.md` gates every version on the one before it, and `v0.2.0` cannot formally open until
`v0.1.x` closes. The `v0.1.x` gate is one sentence: two consecutive weeks with no open contract or
check defect. It has been quietly satisfiable for weeks and was never declared, which left the next
version nominally unopenable while work for it was already being scoped.

Declaring it required answering a question nobody had ruled: **what counts as a defect for the
purpose of that clock?** A site-guard defect was found on 2026-08-22, after the last benchmark fix.
If site guards count, the clock restarts there. If they do not, it starts earlier.

There is a second, larger problem the declaration surfaces, recorded here rather than left for a
later reader to find: the gate does not measure what the version is about.

## Decision

### 1. The clock clause is met

Three candidate clock starts, and the gate clears on all three:

| Clock start | What happened | Days to 2026-09-13 |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-16 (`6fed037`) | The last benchmark defect fix | 28 |
| 2026-08-22 | The site-guard defect | 22 |
| 2026-08-25 (`f191147`) | `main` had no branch protection until this landed, the most adversarial reading of "check defect" available | 19 |

The gate requires 14. The most conservative start clears it by five days. The issue tracker has held
zero open issues throughout the period.

### 2. A site-guard defect counts

The gate says "contract or check defect" and does not qualify which checks. A site guard is a check:
it runs in CI, it can fail, and when it failed it was fixed like any other defect. Excluding it would
require an argument that site guards are a lesser class of check, and no such argument exists in the
repository. So it counts, and it resets the clock.

Ruled now specifically because it is free now. Had the site-guard defect been the difference between
met and not met, any ruling would have been open to the charge that the definition was chosen to
produce the desired answer.

### 3. The declaration is about the clause, not the version

**This ADR does not declare `v0.1.x` complete.** `ROADMAP.md` describes the version's largest piece
as a verification pass with four named items. Two have been done: CI runtime is measured at 34
seconds against an under-four-minute target, and `bench.yml` had its first live dispatch on
2026-08-17. **Two have never been run:**

- **The live planted-failure checks** (S-07 AC-1, tracked as E16). Every CI job that is supposed to
  fail on bad input still only looks correct on inspection.
- **The tag guard from a scratch clone** (S-07 AC-4, tracked as E15). `release.yml`'s version guard
  has never been exercised against a fresh clone.

A two-week quiet period is satisfied by nobody filing anything, and this project's issue tracker has
never had an issue filed in it. **An empty tracker measures reporting volume as much as it measures
defect absence.** Declaring a verification-themed version closed on the strength of a quiet tracker,
while two of its four verifications have never been run, would be the precise failure this
repository documents about itself: self-imposed checkpoints that are written well and never swept at
release time.

So the gate clause is met, and E15 and E16 remain open and are the honest condition for drawing the
`v0.1.x` line.

## Consequences

- **`v0.2.0` is unblocked in sequence.** Combined with the shape ruling recorded in
  [the v0.2.0 shape options brief](../release-plans/v0.2.0-shape-options.md), work tagged `v0.2.0`
  can proceed.
- **E15 and E16 are carried, not cancelled.** They keep their `v0.1.x` tags. Whether they land as a
  final `v0.1.7` or are folded into the next release is a separate call this ADR does not make.
- **The gate's wording is now known to be under-specified**, and re-cutting it is E12's job. A future
  gate that says "no open defect" should say who is expected to file, or it measures silence.
- **Nothing in `ROADMAP.md` changed.** A reader of the public roadmap still sees the v0.1.x section as
  written on 2026-08-23. That gap is deliberate and tracked (E13), not an oversight.
