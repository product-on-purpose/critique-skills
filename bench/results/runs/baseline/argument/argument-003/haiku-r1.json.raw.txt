Location: Section "What the Pilot Showed", second paragraph
Evidence: "A parallel patron survey found that self-reported avoidance of the library due to fear of fines dropped from 22 percent of respondents before the pilot to 5 percent after it, using the same survey instrument both times."
Problem: The survey result is attributed to self-reported avoidance, but the document does not clarify whether this reduction was specifically caused by removing fines or by other factors that occurred during the pilot period.

Location: Section "Why the Pilot Result Applies Systemwide"
Evidence: "Removing the financial penalty for late returns predictably increases both on-time return rates and overall program participation, which is the reason the pilot's return-rate and avoidance-survey results, taken together, support ending fines for the whole consortium rather than only the pilot branches."
Problem: The claim that removing fines "predictably" increases return rates is presented as established fact, but the evidence supporting this comes only from a single 16-month pilot at 5 branches.
Fix: Revise the language to say the pilot's results "suggest" or "indicate" that removing fines may increase return rates, rather than stating it as a predictable outcome.

Location: Section "Addressing the Revenue Concern"
Evidence: "Consortium finance records show fine revenue covered under 2 percent of the annual materials budget over the last three fiscal years, and the four pilot branches absorbed the loss without a measurable change to their replacement-copy spending, so the shortfall the objection anticipates has not materialized where the policy has already been tried."
Problem: The argument uses data from only 4 pilot branches to support a systemwide implementation, but system-wide fine revenue may not be distributed evenly across all 10 branches, so the loss could disproportionately affect branches with different circulation patterns.
Fix: Include data showing how fine revenue is distributed across the 10 branches to demonstrate that the 2 percent average loss can be absorbed uniformly, or acknowledge that some branches may be more heavily impacted than others.

Location: Section "Recommendation", final sentence
Evidence: "This recommendation applies to items returned within 60 days of their due date; items still unreturned past that window remain subject to the existing replacement-cost billing process, matching the return-rate pattern the pilot data actually established."
Problem: The statement says the 60-day cutoff "matches the return-rate pattern the pilot data actually established," but the pilot data section does not specify that the pilot tracked returns beyond 60 days or provide evidence for this threshold.
Fix: Either provide the pilot data showing the 60-day cutoff was determined by actual return rates, or revise the language to say the 60-day window is recommended as a reasonable transition period rather than derived from pilot data.
