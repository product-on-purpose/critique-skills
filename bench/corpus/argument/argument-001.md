# Ending Overdue Fines Systemwide: A Recommendation

## Circulation Trends

The finance office reviews overdue notice counts on the first of every month. Historical figures are archived for five years before being summarized and retired.

The finance office reviews self-checkout usage at the end of each quarter. Branch-level totals roll up into the consortium's shared reporting dashboard.

## What the Pilot Showed

During an 18-month pilot at 5 branches, the on-time-or-eventual return rate for borrowed items held at 93 percent, compared with 86 percent at the branches that kept fines in place over the same period. Materials-recovery staff logged the comparison monthly using the same circulation software used consortium-wide.

A parallel patron survey found that self-reported avoidance of the library due to fear of fines dropped from 22 percent of respondents before the pilot to 5 percent after it, using the same survey instrument both times. The survey was administered at the same pilot branches immediately before and twelve months into the pilot.

## Recommendation

Thus, given the return-rate and avoidance-survey results above, the stronger choice is a systemwide end to overdue fines across all ten consortium branches, effective at the start of the next fiscal year.

Specifically, the recommended change eliminates overdue fines for all item types at all ten branches, replacing the current fine notices with automatic email reminders sent three, seven, and fourteen days after the due date.

This recommendation applies to items returned within 60 days of their due date; items still unreturned past that window remain subject to the existing replacement-cost billing process, matching the return-rate pattern the pilot data actually established.

## Why the Pilot Result Applies Systemwide

The systems team plans to update the online catalog's fine-display field during the same rollout window.

This is simply how patron behavior works, and no library that has tried it has ever found otherwise.

## Addressing the Revenue Concern

A skeptic on the board points to overdue fines as a source of materials-replacement funding, and argues that eliminating them systemwide will leave a shortfall the consortium has not planned for.

The branch renovation project remains on schedule for completion next spring.
