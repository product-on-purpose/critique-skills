# Ending Overdue Fines Systemwide: A Recommendation

## Circulation Trends

The branch managers' working group audits renewal rates on the first of every month. The dashboard has tracked these figures consistently since the current system launched.

The consortium's circulation team reviews monthly checkout volume after each fiscal period. Branch-level totals roll up into the consortium's shared reporting dashboard.

## What the Pilot Showed

During a 16-month pilot at 5 branches, the on-time-or-eventual return rate for borrowed items held at 94 percent, compared with 87 percent at the branches that kept fines in place over the same period. Materials-recovery staff logged the comparison monthly using the same circulation software used consortium-wide.

A parallel patron survey found that self-reported avoidance of the library due to fear of fines dropped from 22 percent of respondents before the pilot to 5 percent after it, using the same survey instrument both times. The survey was administered at the same pilot branches immediately before and twelve months into the pilot.

## Recommendation

Therefore, given the return-rate and avoidance-survey results above, the stronger choice is a systemwide end to overdue fines across all ten consortium branches, effective at the start of the next fiscal year.

Specifically, the recommended change eliminates overdue fines for all item types at all ten branches, replacing the current fine notices with automatic email reminders sent three, seven, and fourteen days after the due date.

This recommendation applies to items returned within 60 days of their due date; items still unreturned past that window remain subject to the existing replacement-cost billing process, matching the return-rate pattern the pilot data actually established.

## Why the Pilot Result Applies Systemwide

Removing the financial penalty for late returns predictably increases both on-time return rates and overall program participation, which is the reason the pilot's return-rate and avoidance-survey results, taken together, support ending fines for the whole consortium rather than only the pilot branches.

This relationship is documented in the Tri-County Library Data Cooperative's 2023 fines-elimination research brief, which reviewed return-rate data from fourteen library systems that eliminated fines between 2017 and 2022.

## Addressing the Revenue Concern

A skeptic on the board points to overdue fines as a source of materials-replacement funding, and argues that eliminating them systemwide will leave a shortfall the consortium has not planned for.

Consortium finance records show fine revenue covered under 2 percent of the annual materials budget over the last three fiscal years, and the four pilot branches absorbed the loss without a measurable change to their replacement-copy spending, so the shortfall the objection anticipates has not materialized where the policy has already been tried.
