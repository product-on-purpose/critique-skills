# TOY rubric source

The toy domain's own criteria, as defined by its injectors in `bench/generator/domains/toy.py` and
specified in `bench/generator/README.md`, "Worked example: the toy domain". This is not a published
external rubric; it is the harness's own worked-example grammar, invented purely to exercise every
part of the domain-plugin API and the skill template end to end. TOY-ACTIVE and TOY-HEDGE are text
injectors; TOY-ORPHAN is a structural injector.

| ID | Operationalization | Operational test | Severity 2 anchor | Severity 3 anchor | Lane | Lane rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TOY-ACTIVE | A paragraph's lead sentence names its actor and states the action directly; the sentence is not recast into a passive construction that deletes who performed it. | Flag a paragraph's lead sentence as a violation when it matches the toy grammar's fixed set of be-plus-participle passive markers (is reviewed, is filed, is closed, is signed off on) with the actor absent. | A single paragraph's lead sentence loses its actor to a passive recast, while the surrounding paragraphs continue naming actors directly. | Every paragraph's lead sentence across an entire section loses its actor to the same passive recast, so a reader cannot identify who performs any action in that section without guessing. | scripted | Detectable by a fixed, closed set of be-plus-participle phrases the toy grammar's own vocabulary defines; no judgment call is needed to find the construction. |
| TOY-HEDGE | A sentence states its point directly; a hedging phrase is not stacked in front of it absent a stated reason for the qualification. | Flag a sentence as a violation when it opens with one of the toy grammar's two fixed hedging phrases (it may possibly be the case that, it could perhaps be argued that), placed immediately ahead of an otherwise direct statement. | One sentence mid-document opens with a stacked hedge, then the rest of the artifact states its points directly. | Every paragraph in a section opens with the same stacked hedge, so a reader must discount every claim in that section before trusting any of it. | scripted | Detectable by a fixed phrase match at the start of a sentence, drawn from a closed, two-item vocabulary; no judgment call is needed. |
| TOY-ORPHAN | A subheading introduces a body of content that follows it before the next heading of equal or higher level begins; a subheading is not left standing with nothing under it. | Flag a heading at level 2 or deeper as a violation when no paragraph block appears between it and the next heading of equal or higher level, or the end of the document, whichever comes first. The document's own level-1 title is exempt, since it heads the whole document rather than introducing a body directly beneath it. | A single orphan subheading appears at the very end of the document, after every substantive section, where a reader's next action is to stop reading anyway. | An orphan subheading appears mid-document, promising a subsection a reader actively navigating by headings expects to find content under, and finds nothing. | scripted | A fixed structural pattern (a heading immediately followed by another heading of equal or higher level, with no intervening paragraph); no judgment call is needed to detect it. |
